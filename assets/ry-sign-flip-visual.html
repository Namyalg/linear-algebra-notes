Why −sin moves to bottom-left in Ry

Rotating about y rotates the zx-plane — but the matrix is indexed in (x, y, z) order.

35°  (0.611 rad)

Left — the zx-plane

Rotating about the y-axis spins the plane containing z and x. Drawn in its own natural order: z first (vertical), x second (horizontal) — the same layout as the familiar 2D R = [[cos,−sin],[sin,cos]] acting on (z,x).

original +z original +x rotated z′ rotated x′
z rotates toward x. In the plane's own (z,x) frame that's the standard 2D rotation — nothing exotic happening geometrically.

Right — same rotation as a matrix

The matrix's rows/columns are forced into (x, y, z) order. That reorders the 2D block: the x row is on top, the z row is on bottom — opposite of the plane's natural (z,x) order on the left.
