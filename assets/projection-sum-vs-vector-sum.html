Two ways to "remove the covered part" of v = (4,5)

u₁ = (1,0) and u₂ = (0,1) are orthogonal. Same target v. Two candidate subtractions — only one removes everything covered.
The core rule: the sum of projections (proju₁ + proju₂) is OK only because u₁ ⟂ u₂. The projection of the vector-sum proj(u₁+u₂) is a DIFFERENT operation that conflates the two directions — always suspect.
Correct — sum of projections: proj_u₁(v) + proj_u₂(v)
proj_u₁(v) = (4,0)  +  proj_u₂(v) = (0,5)
total covered = (4,5)  →  leftover v − (4,5) = (0,0) ✓
(every covered direction peeled separately → nothing left)
(4,0)+(0,5) = (4.5,4.5) + (−0.5,0.5) — keeps BOTH rotated pieces
Wrong — projection of the vector sum: proj_(u₁+u₂)(v)
u₁+u₂ = (1,1);  proj_(1,1)(v) = (9/2)(1,1) = (4.5,4.5)
leftover = (4,5) − (4.5,4.5) = (−0.5, 0.5) ✗
(−0.5,0.5) IS proj_(1,−1)(v) — the anti-diagonal piece this operation DROPS
The exact relationship: the two operations differ by exactly one term.
 proj_u₁(v) + proj_u₂(v)  =  proj_(u₁+u₂)(v) + proj_(u₁−u₂)(v)
     (4,5)           =   (4.5,4.5)     +   (−0.5,0.5)
Sum of projections = the full v (keeps both rotated pieces). Projection of the sum = only the diagonal piece — it drops the anti-diagonal one, and that dropped piece is the entire difference. They're equal only when v lies exactly on the diagonal (anti-diagonal component = 0).
