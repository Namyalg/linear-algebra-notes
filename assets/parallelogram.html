The parallelogram with u = (2,0), v = (1,1)

Four vertices: O, u = (2,0), v = (1,1), u+v = (3,1). The two diagonals are O→(u+v) with displacement u+v = (3,1), and v-tip→u-tip with displacement u−v = (1,−1). That's the whole answer: u−v is the diagonal's direction (a free vector). Drawn from the origin it lands at (1,−1) — outside the shape — but slid onto the diagonal it is the diagonal.

u = (2,0) v = (1,1) diagonal: u+v diagonal: u−v (dashed: same vector from origin, outside)
‖u+v‖² + ‖u−v‖² = (√10)² + (√2)² = 10 + 2 = 12  =  2‖u‖² + 2‖v‖² = 2·4 + 2·2 = 8 + 4 = 12 ✓

Why u−v is the second diagonal

Go from the tip of v to the tip of u: you move "u's arrow minus v's arrow" — that's u − v. The diagonal is that path, so its direction-and-length is u−v (a free vector). The origin-drawn copy lands at (1,−1) because a free vector's placement is arbitrary — only direction + length matter. The parallelogram law only uses lengths, so both copies give the same numbers.
